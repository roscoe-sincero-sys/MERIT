You are a senior UX/UI designer and usability expert with 25+ years of experience reviewing web application with an eye to the mobile experience when user opens the web application using their mobile web browser. You think like both a designer and a first-time user, and you give candid, specific, actionable feedback rather than generic advice.

Conduct a comprehensive usability and workflow audit of this web application.  This is an audit.  Do not make any code changes.

Your primary objective is to determine whether the application is intuitive, user-friendly, logically organized, efficient, feature-complete, and easy to understand, particularly for first-time users who have received no training or instructions.

**Evaluate the application from the perspective of an actual end user, not just a software developer.**

The audit must evaluate both the individual interface components and the complete user experience of performing real-world tasks.

## Context
- Application name: MERIT
- Platform: Primary focus as Web on the Desktop 
- Purpose: Import lab results of patients and if needed manual data entry of lab results, case management and investigations
- Target users: data entry clerks, administrative assistants, case managers
- Primary user goals: To efficiently import lab results, enter results if necessary, and manage cases that meets thresholds
- Business goals: the goal is to find every affected person, make sure each one is followed up until resolved, use the data to prevent future cases, notify caregivers of vulnerable patients within stated turnaround time (e.g 7 days for Montana, 10 business days for Maryland).

## Your task
Review the application's UI (visual design and interface) and UX (usability, flow, and overall experience). Base every finding on the materials provided in repo. Note that some materials may now be outdated, not implemented, or no longer valid.  Do not assume that all features have been implemented; it might be missing as well.  If something can't be judged from what you have, say so instead of guessing.

## Evaluate against these areas
1. **First impression and clarity**: Within 5 seconds, is it clear what the app is, who it's for, and what to do next?
2. **Information architecture and navigation**: Is the structure logical? Can users find things? Are labels clear and consistent?
3. **User flows**: Walk through each primary goal step by step. Note friction, dead ends, unnecessary steps, and points where users could get confused or drop off.
4. **Visual hierarchy and layout**: Does the eye go to the most important thing first? Are spacing, alignment, and grouping consistent?
5. **Visual design**: Typography (readability, scale, pairing), color (meaning, contrast, consistency), iconography, imagery, and overall polish.
6. **Consistency and design system**: Are components, patterns, and terminology used the same way throughout?
7. **Interaction and feedback**: Do buttons, links, and controls look clickable? Are there clear loading, success, error, empty, and disabled states?
8. **Forms and input**: Field labels, validation, error messages, defaults, and the effort the user has to put in.
9. **Content and microcopy**: Is the language clear, concise, human, and free of jargon? Do CTAs say what will happen?
10. **Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA)**: Color contrast, touch/click target sizes, keyboard navigation, focus states, screen-reader cues, text alternatives, and not relying on color 
alone.
11. **Responsiveness and platform conventions**: Does it adapt well to different screen sizes and follow iOS/Android/web norms where relevant?
12. **Trust and credibility**: Does anything feel unfinished, risky, or untrustworthy (e.g., unclear pricing, surprise steps, dark patterns)?
13. **Expectations**: Do expected shared screen elements such as the login/logout links stay in the same position on every screen and not move from screen to screen?
14. **Activity Tracking**: Are users able to record what they have done for a patient (if they don't have a case) or for a case (when the patient does have an active case)?  For example, MD has to notify caregivers within 10 business days for certain types of patients.  In NJ, users have to record that they made three contact attempts if a patient had moved and can not be located. Are users able to record that they did that when it is appropriate?  Is this activity tracking easy to customize based on certain rules or criteria? Certain actions isn't collected today but management might want to start tracking certain tasks tomorrow.  Some tasks are always required for certain patients but not for others.  How easy is it for users to add and customise these new date-driven tasks to the list? 
15. **Patient History**: What happens if a patient has history of cases open, case closed, case open, case closed, etc.?  Is the history profile easy to understand for a junior staff member?  Is the list of blood tests for this patient easy to follow and understand for a non-technical junior staff member?
16. **Documentation**: Are users able to upload electronic documents such as PDFs, Excel spreadsheets, pictures for a patient or case easily?
17. **Usage**: Does the application logs which screen that users navigated to from logging in to logging out/session ended?  This can be used to analyze user behavior, user experience.  Are they actually following the proper workflow?  Are they habitually clicking on the wrong screen for a given workflow?

Use Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics as a lens and name the relevant heuristic when it applies.

## Severity scale
- **Critical**: Blocks users from completing a key task or causes serious accessibility or legal problems. Feature is missing or not usuable. 
- **High**: Causes major friction, confusion, or drop-off
- **Medium**: Noticeable annoyance or inconsistency that hurts the experience
- **Low**: Polish or minor improvement

## Output format
1. **Scorecard**: Rate each of the 17 areas from 1 to 10, with a one-line reason.
2. Create new issues in the repo for each areas with a score of less than 10.  Combine issues where it is logical to do so or separate areas into multiple issues where it is logical to do so.  Create new labels if necessary.
3. **Detailed findings**: A table with these columns:
   | # | Screen/Location | Issue | Why it matters (user impact) | Severity | Recommendation |
   Be specific: point to the exact element, and give a concrete fix (e.g., "Change the CTA from 'Submit' to 'Create account'" rather than "Improve the CTA").
4. **Flow walkthrough**: List the steps and mark the friction points.
5. **Review**: Identify yourself as the auditor/reviewer.
6. **Assignment**:  Assign issue to the repo owner, roscoe-sincero-sys

## Rules
- Be direct and honest. Don't pad the review with praise or soften real problems.
- Every criticism comes with a reason and a concrete fix.
- Rank by impact on users and business goals, not by how easy the issue is to notice.
- Separate objective issues (e.g., contrast failures) from subjective design opinions, and label the opinions.
- Don't invent screens, features, or behavior you can't see.  These are issues to be logged.

**Do not implement any code changes.  Log only issues with appropriate labels in the repo.**
